Where the 4 percent rule breaks

The rule everyone repeats has failure points, and Firenomics walks into them: the math gets shakier as retirement stretches toward 50 years, and a portfolio concentrated in the largest names carries sequence risk that can break a 15-year plan written on average returns. The articles name the decade that does the damage instead of hiding it inside an average.

Withdrawal is a strategy, not a slide

Spending fear shows up in the withdrawal math: the site's piece on withdrawal strategy treats the dread itself as something to be priced, then lays out the mechanics that calm it. Bridge income after a late-career layoff gets its own treatment under involuntary FIRE, because the spreadsheet does not care whether the exit was chosen.

Expected value everywhere

The same EV lens points outward: a $997 guru course gets compared against 15 years of index returns rather than argued about, and a desired salary gets priced like an investor would price it, from a 40 percent savings rate backward. Nothing on firenomics.com asks for your net worth; the method is the product.
